# Request archive (capture transport v2)

Capture is opt-in; client-log imports remain metadata-only. The Activity worker alone owns archive tables in the existing encrypted `activity.db`. The renderer never opens the database.

## Complete downstream capture

The proxy records authenticated downstream HTTP request/response bytes and WebSocket application messages. There is no body-size, message-count, concurrent-capture or unacknowledged-queue eviction limit. Raw compressed and non-UTF-8 bytes remain lossless. Request headers, query strings, provider-hidden content, upstream executor bodies and WebSocket protocol control frames are excluded. Bodies themselves may contain sensitive data.

HTTP records consumed request bytes and successfully written response bytes. Unread requests, cancelled connections, failed writes and handler panics are partial. WebSocket capture covers Responses/Codex connections and direct/realtime sideband relays. Application message type, direction, time, boundaries and order are preserved, including binary messages. It records the observed downstream exchange, not a task-success guarantee.

With recording enabled, the proxy groups writes into chunks of at most 64 KiB and bounded event batches, committing at the size threshold, every three seconds, and before finalization or explicit disable. It synchronously commits those encrypted batches into `captures.db` under its profile's proxy directory. bbolt is already a fork dependency. AES-GCM encrypts each event with a fresh random nonce and authenticated sequence identity. The supervisor derives a separate journal key from the existing persistent database key using HMAC-SHA-256 and the context `spillway.capture-journal.v2`, passes it in `SPILLWAY_CAPTURE_KEY`, and supplies `SPILLWAY_CAPTURE_DIR`. The proxy removes the key from its environment after reading it. No new key is written to disk.

Recording applies backpressure through journal commits instead of retaining an unbounded memory queue or dropping content. Disk/storage failures leave explicit capture errors and partial records; they never certify a complete body. Inference continues on capture storage failure. A physically unavailable disk cannot provide a complete recording. Capture stays enabled until explicitly disabled; consumer stalls do not expire recording. The supervised proxy exits when its parent app exits.

## Journal and acknowledgement

`PUT /v8/management/observability/captures/lease` accepts `{enabled,version:2}` and returns capture protocol `version: 2`, `leaseSeconds: 0` and `chunkBytes`. Enabling requires configured encrypted storage; older binaries are refused by the app with an update error. Disabling stops new recording and finalizes active streams as partial without deleting their committed bytes.

`GET /v8/management/observability/captures?after=N` returns up to four events, `version: 2`, a persistent epoch and an optional storage error. Each capture appends a `start`, zero or more `data` chunks, and one `end`. Chunks carry base64 wire bytes, direction and zero-based byte offset. WebSocket chunks also carry application-message index, type, timestamp and end marker. Terminal metadata carries direction byte counts and SHA-256 digests. Events never contain headers or query strings.

Spillway validates contiguous sequences, offsets, message boundaries, counts and terminal hashes. A malformed v2 page rolls back entirely; its cursor is not advanced. It stays visibly blocked until the producer/storage fault is repaired or the user explicitly clears it, preserving potentially recoverable source bytes. Each page, raw zstd pieces, metadata, export references and cursor commit together in the encrypted SQLite database using WAL + FULL synchronization. Only then does `POST /observability/captures/ack` release journal events through `{epoch,sequence}`. Duplicate pages and acknowledgements are harmless. An acknowledgement interrupted after commit is retried even when the next page is empty. Epoch mismatches and future acknowledgements are rejected.

Closed streams whose final commit failed are retried on journal polling or recording renewal, so transient storage failures cannot leave them pending indefinitely. Journal epoch/sequence survive proxy restarts. Unfinished streams receive a partial terminal event on recovery; already-committed bytes remain available. An abrupt process/power interruption can lose the bounded, uncommitted suffix and is never called complete. A changed encryption key or damaged journal causes an explicit recovery error without replacing queued data. The sync loop drains four pages per pass, yields to the event loop before another full pass and otherwise polls every three seconds. Turning recording off still drains previously committed v2 events.

Explicit user Clear disables capture, stops/drains the in-flight sync operation and deletes local archive content immediately. A durable clear marker fences ingestion until the proxy journal is cleared and its epoch rotates. Stopping capture and normal retention are not Clear. Activity deletion and eval retention keep their established separate policies. When the proxy is offline, local deletion succeeds and the UI reports pending journal deletion. The next successful connection clears that journal before importing anything; cleared bodies cannot reappear.

## Local records and readers

`ArchiveEntry.schemaVersion` remains 1. Identity is `trace:<full inbound trace ID>`, independent of Activity row IDs and short log identifiers. Capture UUID, epoch and sequence retain provenance. Model/provider/session/client come only from correlated Activity events. A trace may have several upstream attempts; up to 200 retained links preserve their own provenance.

`archive_wire` indexes message continuations by entry, message index and sequence; chunk writes update thread accounting incrementally instead of rescanning the capture. It references independent, immutable raw-byte pieces in `archive_pieces`; encrypted SQLite protects bytes, metadata and search. `archive_entries.detail` marks these captures with `wire: 2` and preserves encoding labels, byte counts and digests. Legacy text/diff captures and dictionary dependencies remain readable without rewriting their historical content. Legacy captures may contain the original 8 MiB/128-message gaps; missing bytes cannot be recovered.

`archiveObserve` records correlated attempt metadata. `archiveIngest` commits a page transactionally. Metadata queries page 50 identities, with a stable `[at,id]` cursor and existing search/dimension/time filters. `archiveStatus` reports policy, stored/logical bytes, sequence and diagnostics. `archiveDetail` provides a bounded decoded display preview. Previews are limited to 8 MiB per direction and 128 displayed messages, marked `previewOnly` when limited; these limits never truncate storage or ClickHouse export. Opaque/binary previews use base64. Full-text search indexes the bounded preview. Diagnostic bundles remain deliberately redacted and bounded, and mark limited previews partial. A limited preview cannot become a replay case.

The inspector renders masked Solid text with incremental display. Email masking affects the screen, not original exported bodies. Raw portable exports use version 2 chunk records; legacy-only or metadata-only selections retain portable version 1.

## Retention and export ownership

Default policy remains off, 7 days and a 100 MiB local body budget; supported settings remain 1–90 days and 1–1024 MiB. Completed local captures follow retention. Active captures, the newest retained conversation and bodies waiting for confirmed ClickHouse delivery can exceed the body-budget target. A large conversation no longer becomes permanently metadata-only. Metadata retention protects identities with pending bodies; old unpinned identities remain bounded to 20,000.

Queued body references pin their owning thread until ClickHouse acknowledges delivery. Backfill also protects retained source content while references are being queued. Disabling capture or losing the destination does not strip queued bodies. The outbox does not evict unacknowledged facts at its former 1 GiB threshold; backfill uses that threshold only to pace preparation. Explicit queue/archive Clear and destination changes retain their documented user-directed semantics. After acknowledgement, local retention resumes. Physical databases may retain reusable free pages and SQLite/FTS/index overhead beyond logical budget accounting.

States: unknown identity; missing/unavailable; pending stream; partial interrupted recording; complete finalized exchange with verified bytes; expired by policy. Complete describes observed bytes, not provider-hidden or semantic completeness. Storage exhaustion, transport interruption, explicit deletion and post-delivery retention are the remaining loss triggers and are represented explicitly.
